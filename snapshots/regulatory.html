Regulatory Hub

What is changing in payments regulation, and when

We track the payments regulation that matters to our clients in the GCC, the UK and Europe, and advise on the practical and commercial response.

Last reviewed: 27 September 2026

Europe

In progress

PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation (PSR)

Provisional agreement was reached in November 2025 and the final compromise texts went to EU member states for approval in April 2026; formal adoption and publication follow. The PSR will apply directly and PSD3 must be written into national law, with most provisions expected to apply from 2028. The package covers fraud liability, verification of payee, open banking access and payment institution licensing.

Expected to apply from 2028 · Read full analysis →

Active

EU Instant Payments Regulation

Payment service providers in the euro area had to be able to receive instant euro credit transfers from January 2025 and to send them, with verification of payee, from October 2025. Later deadlines apply to member states outside the euro area and to payment and e-money institutions.

In force since October 2025

Deferred

EU AI Act: high-risk system obligations

The AI Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the start of high-risk obligations from August 2026 to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems, which include creditworthiness assessment and credit scoring, and to 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems. Conformity assessment, technical documentation and human oversight still need to be planned well ahead.

Applies from 2 December 2027 · Read full analysis →

In progress

Digital Euro Regulation

The European Parliament's economic affairs committee approved its position on the digital euro regulation on 23 June 2026. Negotiations with EU member states follow, with agreement targeted by the end of 2026 and first issuance expected around 2029. The regulation will set holding limits, distribution through payment service providers and compensation rules.

Council negotiations under way · Read full analysis →

Active

ISO 20022 on SWIFT

The SWIFT coexistence period for cross-border payment instructions ended in November 2025, and ISO 20022 (MX) is now the required format. Institutions that still depend on translation lose structured data such as addresses and purpose codes, which makes screening harder.

In effect since November 2025

Active

EU AML Package and AMLA

The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) started work in Frankfurt in July 2025. The single EU AML rulebook applies from 10 July 2027, and AMLA is due to begin direct supervision of selected high-risk institutions from 2028.

Single rulebook applies July 2027

United Kingdom

Active

FCA CASS 15: Safeguarding for Payment and E-Money Firms

Since 7 May 2026, payment and e-money institutions must follow the FCA's new safeguarding rules: daily internal and external reconciliations, a monthly safeguarding return from June 2026, annual safeguarding audits for most firms and a resolution pack to help return customer funds on insolvency.

In force since 7 May 2026

Active

UK APP Fraud Reimbursement

Since 7 October 2024, sending and receiving payment firms share equally the cost of reimbursing victims of authorised push payment (APP) scams on Faster Payments, up to £85,000 per claim.

In force since October 2024 · Read full analysis →

In progress

Payment Systems Regulator to Merge into the FCA

The Financial Services and Markets Bill 2026, introduced in the House of Lords on 19 May 2026, would abolish the Payment Systems Regulator and transfer its functions to the FCA. The change is not expected to take effect before 2028.

Bill before Parliament

In progress

Cross-Border Card Interchange: PSR Price Cap

In January 2026 the High Court rejected a challenge by Visa, Mastercard and Revolut to the PSR's power to cap UK–EEA card-not-present interchange fees. The PSR has dropped plans for an interim cap and is working on the method for a longer-term cap.

Methodology in development · Read full analysis →

GCC

Active

CBB Bahrain Rulebook: PSP, AISP and Crypto Module Updates

The Central Bank of Bahrain updated Rulebook Volumes 1, 2, 5 and 6 in May 2026, introducing revised licensing requirements for PSP, AISP and PSIP applicants, updated client money account rules, and amendments to the Crypto-Asset Module. BPay Global, a Binance Group entity, received a PSP licence under the updated framework.

Effective May 2026

ImminentNEW

CBUAE AlTareq Open Finance: September 2026 Compliance Deadline

Under Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 and CBUAE Circular 3 of 2025, affected entities face a key compliance deadline of 16 September 2026. The framework uses a centralised API hub operated by CBUAE entity Nebras, with Lean Technologies and Wio Bank already live under the operational AlTareq infrastructure.

Compliance deadline 16 September 2026

In Progress

QCB Open Banking Interoperability and Consent Standards 2026

Qatar Central Bank is scaling open banking from sandbox to production in 2026, publishing formal interoperability standards and a unified consent model governing API connections between banks and third-party providers. QNB has launched live account-information and payment-initiation APIs, with standards expected in Q3 to Q4 2026.

Standards expected Q3-Q4 2026

Active

UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025: Open Finance as Licensed Activity

Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 brings open finance within the Central Bank of the UAE's licensing framework (Article 61). Firms covered by the law had until 15 September 2026 to bring their activities into line with it.

In force since 16 September 2025

Active

QCB: Direct PSP Access to QA-RTGS

On 1 September 2026 Qatar Central Bank allowed payment service providers to open accounts in, and settle directly through, its real-time gross settlement system QA-RTGS, without an intermediary bank. SADAD Payment Solutions and Dibsy were the first two PSPs to join.

Live since September 2026

Active

Qatar: Fawran Instant Payments

Qatar Central Bank's Fawran instant payment system has been live since early 2024, allowing account-to-account transfers around the clock using mobile numbers or aliases. Since October 2024 it has also been available for online purchases through the national QPay e-commerce gateway.

Live since 2024 · Read full analysis →

In progress

Qatar: Open Banking Roadmap

Qatar Central Bank's FinTech Strategy (2023–2028) sets out an open banking roadmap. Payment initiation and data-sharing services are being tested through the QCB regulatory sandbox, but a standalone open banking regulation has not yet been issued.

Regulation not yet issued · Read full analysis →

Active

SAMA: Open Banking Licensing

The Saudi Central Bank has moved open banking from its sandbox to formal licensing. In March 2026 Lean Technologies became the first company licensed to provide open banking services in Saudi Arabia.

Licensing since March 2026 · Read full analysis →

Active

SAMA: Fees Guide for Financial Institutions' Services

Announced in December 2025 to replace the Banking Tariff, the guide lowers maximum fees for services including international purchases and cash withdrawals, mada card reissuance, and transfers from bank accounts and e-wallets. It applies to banks and payment companies supervised by SAMA.

Replaces the Banking Tariff

Active

UAE: New Central Bank Law

Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 replaced the 2018 Central Bank Law and brought banking, insurance and payments under one framework, including technology providers that facilitate financial services. The one-year transition period for existing firms ended in mid-September 2026.

Transition period ended September 2026

Active

UAE: Open Finance and AlTareq

Under the CBUAE Open Finance Regulation, banks and insurers connect to licensed third-party providers through the central Nebras API hub, branded AlTareq for customers. Banks are going live in stages; ADIB became the first Islamic bank to implement it in January 2026. The framework covers data sharing, payment initiation and the initiation of other financial services, and has been extended to insurance.

Phased roll-out · Read full analysis →

Active

UAE: Payment Token Services Regulation

Issued by the CBUAE in 2024, the regulation requires a licence to issue dirham-backed stablecoins and restricts the use of foreign payment tokens in the UAE to buying virtual assets.

In force since 2024 · Read full analysis →

Active

Kuwait: National Payments System Upgrade

The Central Bank of Kuwait launched the Kuwait Automated Clearing House (KACH) on 5 January 2026 and the Kuwait Dispute Management System (KDMS) on 2 February 2026, giving payment system participants a central platform for clearing and for resolving transaction disputes.

Live since February 2026

Global

Active

Project Aperta: Cross-Border Open Finance

A BIS Innovation Hub project with the Central Bank of the UAE, Banco Central do Brasil, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the UK's FCA built a prototype layer linking national open finance networks without changing them. The final report was published on 29 May 2026 and the CBUAE announced completion on 17 June 2026.

Completed June 2026

Active

FATF Travel Rule: Cross-Border Transfers and Virtual Assets

FATF Recommendation 16 requires originator and beneficiary information to travel with cross-border wire transfers, and applies the same principle to virtual asset transfers. Uneven implementation between countries still creates gaps for firms sending to jurisdictions that have not yet applied it.

Ongoing implementation

Active

Visa Acquirer Monitoring Programme (VAMP)

Visa's VAMP, introduced in 2025, combines fraud and dispute ratios for card-not-present transactions into one measure for merchants and acquirers. Thresholds tighten during 2026, and merchants above them face fees and remediation requirements.

Thresholds tightening in 2026
